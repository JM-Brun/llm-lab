# Reusable reference-page template

Use this archetype when readers need to **consult, compare, or situate information** rather than follow a single explanatory argument. Suitable pages include histories, timelines, taxonomies, catalogues, and source collections.

## Suggested structure

1. **Scope and selection criteria** — Explain what is included, excluded, and why.
2. **Conventions** — Define terminology, date conventions, units, categories, or comparison criteria.
3. **Main reference content** — Timeline, taxonomy, table, catalogue, or structured list.
4. **Interpretation and caveats** — Explain what the material does and does not imply. Keep secondary historiographical or methodological caveats collapsed when they are not needed to read the main content.
5. **Sources and provenance** — Prefer primary sources for historical and technical claims; identify uncertain or disputed dates and attributions.
6. **Connections** — Link back to overview pages and onward to detailed concept pages.

## Compact authoring skeleton

```markdown
A short introduction states the scope and selection principle.

## Conventions

Explain how to read dates, categories, units, or labels.

## Timeline / taxonomy / catalogue

Present the reference material with direct links to authoritative sources.

:::
```

## Editorial rules

- Distinguish a selective reference from an exhaustive inventory.
- Separate chronological order from causal influence. A timeline alone does not establish causation or priority.
- Distinguish publication, submission, announcement, and release dates when relevant.
- Make comparison criteria explicit; do not imply equivalence from superficial similarity.
- Cite the source for each material historical or factual claim, not only in a bibliography at the end.
- Keep source provenance visible and avoid presenting uncertain claims as settled facts.
