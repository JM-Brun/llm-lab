# Reference-page quick start

Use this pattern when readers primarily need to **look up, compare, or situate information**, rather than follow one continuous explanatory argument. Typical cases include glossaries, taxonomies, timelines, catalogues, bibliographies, and comparison tables.

The [flexible page-template system](page-template-system.md) remains the shared composition guide. This page adds prompts specific to reliable reference material.

## Questions worth answering

- What is included, excluded, and according to which selection criteria?
- Which terms, dates, units, categories, or comparison dimensions need explicit conventions?
- How should the reader scan, filter, compare, or interpret the material?
- Which facts are uncertain, disputed, version-dependent, or incomplete?
- Which primary or official sources support the entries?

## Choose the representation

Use the structure that makes retrieval easiest: a concise definition list, table, taxonomy, chronology, annotated bibliography, or structured catalogue. Do not add an explanatory chapter around data that is already self-explanatory; do add interpretation when it prevents a misleading reading.

## Reliability safeguards

- Distinguish a selective list from an exhaustive inventory.
- Separate chronology from causation and publication, submission, announcement, and release dates.
- Make comparison criteria explicit; superficial similarity does not establish equivalence.
- Cite material claims at the point where they appear and retain provenance for uncertain or disputed entries.
- Keep conventions close to the table or figure they govern.
- Link to explanatory pages when a reader needs to understand why an item matters; do not turn the reference into a duplicate tutorial.

A reference entry may be very short. Do not add a self-check, learning-outcomes block, or “further reading” section unless it improves the lookup task.
