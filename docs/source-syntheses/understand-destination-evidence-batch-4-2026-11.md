**Declared purpose:** transform logits into a next-token distribution and distinguish normalization from calibration.

| Local contribution | Claim to preserve | Provenance | FR destination | EN destination | Presence and decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `probability-ic-01-softmax-normalizes-vocabulary-scores` | Softmax maps the vocabulary logits at one position to positive values summing to one. | `probability-ic-02-next-token-probability-is-prefix-conditional` | The distribution is conditional on the supplied prefix. | `probability-ic-03-normalization-is-not-calibration-or-truth` | A normalized distribution is not automatically calibrated and does not establish factual truth. ## 3. Entropy — `entropy` {#entropy}

**Page identity:** `site/fr/entropy.qmd` / `site/en/entropy.qmd`  
**Declared purpose:** compare distributions over the same vocabulary and distinguish expected uncertainty from one observed token's surprisal.

| Local contribution | Claim to preserve | Provenance | FR destination | EN destination | Presence and decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `entropy-ic-01-whole-distribution-expected-surprisal` | Predictive entropy is the expectation of surprisal over the entire next-token distribution. For a finite vocabulary of size `V`, it lies between zero and `log V` nats when natural logarithms are used. | Shannon's information-theoretic definition, cited in the lesson; the finite-vocabulary bound follows from the discrete entropy maximum. | [Entropy of the next-token distribution](../../site/fr/entropy.qmd#entropie-de-la-distribution-du-prochain-token); [numerical example](../../site/fr/entropy.qmd#exemple-numérique-la-distribution-du-scribe) | [Entropy of the next-token distribution](../../site/en/entropy.qmd#entropy-of-the-next-token-distribution); [numerical example](../../site/en/entropy.qmd#numerical-example-the-roman-questions-next-token-distribution) | Explicit in both languages, including the worked four-token example and matching entropy value. Keep the unit (nats) and finite-vocabulary assumption. |
| `entropy-ic-02-predictive-entropy-is-not-observed-surprisal` | Entropy describes expected surprisal before observing the next token. Once a target is observed, its surprisal is `-log p(target | prefix)`; for a one-hot target this is also token NLL / the cross-entropy contribution. | Shannon's definition plus the project lesson's explicit connection to autoregressive scoring and one-hot cross-entropy. | [Predictive entropy and observed token](../../site/fr/entropy.qmd#entropie-prédictive-et-token-observé-deux-questions-différentes) | [Predictive entropy and the observed token](../../site/en/entropy.qmd#predictive-entropy-and-the-observed-token-are-different-questions) | Explicit in both languages. This distinction is essential to prevent confusion between uncertainty over all candidates and the score of one observed target. |
| `entropy-ic-03-low-entropy-does-not-imply-correctness` | Low entropy can coexist with a confidently wrong prediction; high entropy does not by itself imply a false continuation. Entropy and post-temperature entropy are not automatically calibrated uncertainty estimates. | Standard information-theoretic/statistical interpretation, bounded in the lesson; the numerical counterexample is project-derived. | [What entropy does not establish](../../site/fr/entropy.qmd#ce-que-lentropie-ne-permet-pas-de-conclure); [entropy and temperature](../../site/fr/entropy.qmd#entropie-température-et-génération) | [What entropy does not establish](../../site/en/entropy.qmd#what-entropy-does-not-establish); [entropy and temperature](../../site/en/entropy.qmd#entropy-temperature-and-generation) | Explicit in both languages. Preserve the distinction between dispersion, calibration and factual correctness. |
| `entropy-ic-04-entropy-likelihood-and-cross-entropy-have-distinct-roles` | Entropy concerns the predictive distribution; likelihood scores observed sequence tokens under reference prefixes; cross-entropy compares a target distribution with a prediction. These quantities are related but not interchangeable. | Project-derived synthesis across the canonical probability, likelihood and cross-entropy lessons; Shannon (1948) supports the entropy definition, not the full LLM-specific distinction. | [Observed-token distinction](../../site/fr/entropy.qmd#entropie-prédictive-et-token-observé-deux-questions-différentes); [what entropy does not establish](../../site/fr/entropy.qmd#ce-que-lentropie-ne-permet-pas-de-conclure) | [Observed-token distinction](../../site/en/entropy.qmd#predictive-entropy-and-the-observed-token-are-different-questions); [what entropy does not establish](../../site/en/entropy.qmd#what-entropy-does-not-establish) | Explicit through direct links to the canonical likelihood and cross-entropy lessons. Do not duplicate their derivations in the entropy lesson. |

**Practice destination:** [FR](../../site/fr/exercises/entropy.qmd) / [EN](../../site/en/exercises/entropy.qmd). Both companions contain a self-check, a token-surprisal calculation and a sequence-scoring transfer task. The exercise links back to the likelihood lesson for the sequence factorization; this is a deliberate cross-branch transfer, not duplicated theory.

**Placement decision:** keep the entropy definition, expected-surprisal interpretation, observed-token contrast and bounded interpretation in Understand. Keep calculations and transfer questions in Practice. Keep compression, V-information, KL derivations and advanced information-theoretic experiments in the existing Deepen resources.

## 4. Cross-page decisions and route continuity

- The sequence `logits → probabilities → entropy` is a coherent prediction-stage progression: unnormalized scores, normalized conditional distribution, then a summary of the distribution's spread.
- The entropy lesson introduces observed-token surprisal only as a contrast. The canonical likelihood lesson owns sequence factorization and perplexity; cross-entropy owns the target-distribution objective. This prevents the prediction-stage route from absorbing the scoring branch.
- The probability lesson points to generation to show what selection and feedback do after a distribution exists. This is a cross-reference to a sibling use, not a prerequisite edge from entropy to generation.
- Temperature is introduced as a transformation of the distribution, while its detailed decoding-policy treatment remains in Deepen. The route should not make temperature a required stage between softmax and generation.
- The FR/EN lesson pairs expose matching conceptual claims, worked examples and Practice destinations. The bilingual terminology differs appropriately without changing the mathematical scope.
- No new concept, route stage, computational edge, page-map field, page, or visual asset is required by this batch.
